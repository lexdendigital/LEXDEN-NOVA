## 1. Principle

NOVA wants buyers to receive what they paid for. This policy explains the operational process and does not remove a statutory consumer protection that cannot lawfully be excluded.

## 2. Start with the seller when practical

For ordinary issues, the buyer should use the support channel shown on the product listing and give the seller a reasonable opportunity to correct a genuine problem.

NOVA may step in earlier for suspected fraud, serious safety issues, non-delivery, inaccessible digital content, major misrepresentation, seller silence or other material concerns.

## 3. Physical goods

Depending on the facts, a buyer may be entitled to repair, replacement, return, refund or another remedy where goods are defective, unsafe, materially not as described, unsuitable for a specifically communicated purpose, of an unacceptable quality or otherwise covered by applicable law.

Where the law provides a right to cancel an advance order/booking or return goods, the seller's product policy cannot remove that right.

## 4. Digital downloads and eBooks

A refund or other remedy may be appropriate where a file is not delivered, inaccessible, corrupted, materially incomplete, materially different from the listing, unlawfully sold or otherwise covered by law.

A blanket "all digital sales are final" statement must not be used to defeat a mandatory legal remedy. The exact effect of access/consumption of digital content depends on the applicable law and facts of the transaction.

## 5. Software

A refund or corrective remedy may be appropriate where software is materially defective, malicious, substantially non-functional as described or otherwise subject to a legal remedy.

## 6. External courses / external access

A remedy may be appropriate where promised access is never supplied, a reasonable access problem is not corrected, the service is materially different from the listing or another legal remedy applies.

A functioning web URL is not, by itself, proof that the buyer received the promised course/content.

## 7. Services

Depending on the failure, a remedy may include correction, completion, replacement of a deficient deliverable, partial refund or full refund.

## 8. Live programmes

Cancellation/refund treatment depends on the programme terms disclosed before purchase, when performance began, whether the programme was materially delivered and applicable law.

## 9. How to submit a dispute

Send the order reference, product, problem description, relevant dates and supporting evidence to lexdendigital@gmail.com or use the order-support flow where available.

## 10. Investigation

NOVA may review payment records, order state, product/version snapshots, delivery events, digital-access events, communications, seller evidence and applicable policies.

NOVA may temporarily pause a listing or payout while a serious dispute is being investigated.

## 11. Refund mechanics

Where a refund is approved for a NOVA checkout transaction, NOVA will normally initiate it through the payment provider/payment route used for the transaction, subject to provider processing and applicable law.

Refund timing can vary. Payment-provider processing charges may not be recoverable from the provider and do not automatically determine the buyer's statutory remedy.

## 12. Chargebacks

A buyer may also have rights through their card/payment network or provider. NOVA and the seller may need to provide transaction, delivery or fulfilment evidence.

## 13. Abuse

NOVA may investigate fraudulent or abusive refund activity. Genuine complaints are not punishable simply because they are inconvenient to a seller.

## 14. Appeals

Where an operational decision is reviewable, the buyer or Creator may submit additional evidence through support.

## 15. ADR / escalation

NOVA may use a staged process:

1. support/complaint intake;
2. seller/platform investigation;
3. escalation review;
4. mediation or another suitable ADR mechanism where available and appropriate;
5. courts or another competent forum where necessary.

Mandatory statutory rights and jurisdictional rules always take precedence.
